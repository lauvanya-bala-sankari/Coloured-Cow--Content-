congratulations. having started as a small idea into a working product that is running for months and having real users. your product worked and people are depending on it. That's real and huge! but for a product that has reached this stage may also experience many problems along the way to actually reach that visionary next stage of the product. some of them are components breaking, integrations giving away, shipping getting slower and much more.to be more precise. Check if you have encountered any of the situations below ,
      
 1, having a half second flinch before approving a change that used to be routine.
 
 2,sitting in a meeting and realizing mid-sentence that you can actually explain what these two parts of your product do, but cant explain  why touching one quietly affects the other .
 
 3,its your best engineer saying 'let me check' about the system they built .
 
 4,Features that used to ship in days now take weeks — not because anyone got slower, but because every change has to be traced through parts of the system
 
 5,new people taking more time to become useful 
 
yeah, we know everything here is fixable on your own but one important and distinguishable question you may ask yourself is "will this system (as it is now) carry platform where you wanted it to go?" if the answer is no, then you are probably experiencing "the problem of legibility". 

the classic sign is everybody can explain their own parts but no one could explain how all of this will fit together. What is lacking is the shared understanding of the product itself.

We saw an exact version of this in Plio — an education platform (https://www.plio.in/) we partnered with from 2021 to 2023. As a working product with real users, plio already proved its idea but as an evolving product, they could recognize the symptom of a legibility issue. the clearest sign showed up in their dashboards. while walking us through the dashboard they built, we could observe that same numbers meant different things to different people in the room. Like, for some the numbers were about running the operation day to day and  For others they were about judging whether things were going well. Nobody was reading them wrong. They were not reading the same thing. A Classic sign!

So, its clear that the problem is more of a clarity problem than it was a technology problem. So, we started with design, instead of code which is why in the first month, we produced more of a description than features. To be precise, it was the description of the product, the first designs and the technical specifications underneath them, including the database schema. To build that new schema, we tried to understand existing first, so we went through it field by field, workflow by workflow, asking one question of each part 'what is this for?' surprisingly, Only two answers kept coming back, this isn't needed at all. Or, this needs a simpler form. Explaining out loud and simplifying the overworked things became the effective fixes here !

As the product evolves, the shared understanding of it devolves! you are not behind. Making the system explainable before making it bigger is the fix.
